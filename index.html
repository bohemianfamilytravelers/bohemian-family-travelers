Travel protection

Protect the trip you've been saving for

Travel protection covers the things nobody plans for. For families and groups paying deposits months ahead, it can be the difference between losing your money and rebooking.

Cancellations

A sick child, a family emergency, or a job change can cancel a trip. Many plans refund prepaid, nonrefundable costs for covered reasons.

Medical care away from home

Your health insurance may not cover you in another country, and emergency care or evacuation can cost thousands.

Delays and missed departures

A late flight can mean missing your cruise. Coverage can help with hotels, meals, and catching up to the ship.

Lost or delayed bags

Help replacing essentials so the trip doesn't stop while the airline finds your luggage.

How we choose your plan

The right protection depends on how you book. For some cruises and resort packages, the supplier's own plan works best. For others, a separate plan gives more coverage. We'll recommend the best fit for your trip.

Some benefits are only available if you buy soon after your first deposit, so ask us early.

Travel document basics

What your family needs to bring

Passports for kids

Every traveler, babies included, needs their own passport for international flights. Child passports (under 16) last 5 years and usually need both parents to apply. Start a few months ahead.

Closed-loop cruises

Sailing round trip from a U.S. port to the Bahamas, Caribbean, Mexico, Canada, or Bermuda? U.S. citizens can usually use a birth certificate, plus photo ID for ages 16 and up. We still recommend passports in case you need to fly home from a foreign port.

Flying to Mexico or the Caribbean

International flights require a passport book. We recommend at least 6 months left before it expires, and checking it as soon as you book.

Puerto Rico & the U.S. Virgin Islands

No passport needed for U.S. citizens. Bring the same ID you'd use for a flight within the U.S.

REAL ID for U.S. flights

Since May 7, 2025, adults 18 and older need a REAL ID–compliant license (look for the star) or a passport at airport security. TSA doesn't require ID for kids under 18 traveling with an adult.

Kids traveling with one parent

Bring a signed consent letter from the parent who isn't traveling. Some countries, airlines, and cruise lines ask for one, and a few cruise lines require passports for kids sailing with one adult.

Rules change. We'll confirm the requirements for your trip, and you can always check travel.state.gov and tsa.gov.
